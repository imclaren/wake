Stay awake. Even with the lid closed.

wake is a Mac menu bar app that keeps your Mac awake for as long as you choose. Keep it awake while the lid is open, or keep a MacBook awake after you close the lid, so that a download, a build or a backup runs on until you turn it off.

{{if .Released}}Download for Mac{{end}} How it works

How it works

A switch in the menu bar, and a choice of how the Mac stays awake and for how long.

While the lid is open

The Mac stays awake while you use it or leave it, and sleeps as usual when you close the lid. This is the way wake starts, and it needs no password.

Even when the lid is closed

It is closing the lid that puts a Mac to sleep, and a Mac that is awake once the lid is closed stays awake. wake turns sleep off only while the lid is open and on again the moment it closes, which leaves the Mac awake with nothing left changed.

For as long as you choose

Keep the Mac awake until you turn wake off, or for one, two, four or eight hours. Keep the display on as well, or let it turn off while the Mac carries on.

Asks for your password once

Keeping a Mac awake with its lid closed changes a setting that only an administrator may change. The first time you choose it, wake says why, then shows the system's own password dialog. It writes a rule that lets your account change that one setting from then on, and never asks again.

Off means asleep

Turn wake off while the lid is closed and the Mac goes to sleep at once, as it would have when you closed the lid. Quitting the app does not put the Mac to sleep.

Nothing collected

wake keeps nothing about you and sends nothing anywhere. The only connection it makes is to this site, to check for a new version.

Mind the heat

A MacBook that is awake inside a bag can get hot. Be careful when you pack one away with wake keeping it awake.

Get wake

For a Mac running macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. Keeping it awake with the lid closed needs a MacBook. It updates itself.
